📖 Understanding Your Results

Final Balance

The total amount in your account at the end of the investment period. This includes your initial principal, all monthly contributions, and the compound interest earned over time.

Total Interest Earned

The total compound interest earned on your principal and contributions. This is your final balance minus the total amount you actually deposited (principal + all monthly contributions).

Effective Annual Rate

The actual annual return you earn, taking into account the effect of compounding frequency. The more frequently interest is compounded, the higher the effective annual rate will be compared to the stated nominal rate.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What is compound interest?

Compound interest is the interest you earn on both your original money (principal) and the interest that has already been added to your account. Over time, this "interest on interest" can significantly grow your savings, making it a powerful tool for long-term wealth building.

How is compound interest calculated?

The formula used is A = P(1 + r/n)^(n*t) + PMT * ((1 + r/n)^(n*t) - 1) / (r/n). Don't worry about the math — this calculator does it for you! It considers your initial principal (P), monthly contributions (PMT), annual interest rate (r), compounding frequency (n), and investment period (t).

What is the difference between nominal rate and effective annual rate?

Nominal rate is the stated annual interest rate (e.g., 7% in the calculator above). Effective annual rate (EAR) is the actual return you earn after accounting for compounding frequency. For example, a 7% nominal rate compounded monthly gives you an EAR of 7.23%, meaning you earn slightly more than 7% per year.

How does compounding frequency affect my returns?

More frequent compounding generates higher returns. For example, a $10,000 investment at 7% interest over 10 years would grow to $19,671 with annual compounding, but to $20,096 with monthly compounding. The difference becomes larger over longer periods.

How does monthly contribution impact my final balance?

Consistent monthly contributions can have a dramatic impact. In the example above, $10,000 invested at 7% for 10 years with no monthly contributions grows to ~$20,096. Adding $500/month increases the final balance to ~$106,639 — over 5x larger!
