📖 How to read your results

▶

Final Balance

Your total account value at the end. Includes principal, all contributions, and compounded interest.

Total Interest

All the interest you earned. Final balance minus what you actually deposited (principal + contributions).

Effective Annual Rate

Your true yearly return after compounding. More frequent compounding means this number is slightly higher than the nominal rate.

❓ FAQ

▶
▶ What is compound interest?
Compound interest is interest calculated on the initial principal and also on the accumulated interest from previous periods. This means you earn interest on your interest, making your money grow faster over time than simple interest.
▶ What is the difference between compound interest and simple interest?
Simple interest is calculated only on the original principal amount. Compound interest is calculated on the principal plus any accumulated interest from previous periods, leading to exponential growth over time.
▶ How often should interest be compounded?
More frequent compounding (daily vs. annually) yields slightly higher returns because interest is added to your account more often. Most savings accounts compound daily or monthly.
▶ How much will I have if I save $500 a month for 30 years at 7% interest?
If you save $500 per month at a 7% annual return, compounded monthly for 30 years, you would have approximately $566,764. Your total contributions would be $180,000 and you would have earned about $386,764 in interest.
